# Licensing and external material

MineCode's own code and documentation use the [MIT License](../LICENSE). You may use, modify and redistribute them under its terms, preserving the required copyright and permission notice. The README's responsible-use requests are community values, not additional license conditions.

Minecraft, mappings, assets, provider software and retrieved references retain their own terms. MineCode's MIT license doesn't relicense them. See [third-party notices](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).

## References and reuse

Public visibility isn't sufficient permission to copy or redistribute material. Record its source, revision, applicable notices and intended use. Review code, documentation, assets and subcomponents separately where their terms differ.

Registry reuse flags are conservative review decisions for the proposed use, not a claim that every upstream license prohibits reuse. Unknown rights mean reference-only until reviewed. A repository badge doesn't license every discussion, dependency or asset.

Keep required notices and attribution. Don't silently copy copyleft or custom-licensed implementations into projects with incompatible distribution terms. Generated code directly derived from external implementation still needs review.

## Contributions

Contribute original work you have the right to share under MIT. Identify and preserve any separately licensed material rather than placing MineCode's license over it. The small Yarn mapping fixtures retained in the test suite have their own CC0 provenance documented alongside them.
